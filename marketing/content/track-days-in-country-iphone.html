Count the dates you can account for

In Whereday, open Timeline → Country Days to count local dates represented by your recorded visits, route points, and manual stays. Choose this year, the last 365 days, or all time, then open a country to review its dates. Viewing the counts and adding manual stays are free. From version 1.8, new users retain exports after tracking access expires; existing free users keep their previous export-unlock requirement.

This guide covers Country Days in Whereday 1.7.16. It is a personal travel journal: its rules do not determine tax residency, immigration status, or how a visa counts a day.

1. Review the dates behind each total

  1. Open Timeline, then Country Days.
  2. Select a period. The default is this year; the other choices are the last 365 days and all time.
  3. Open a country and review the individual dates. A total is only useful when you understand which dates produced it.
  4. If older locations lack countries, use the analysis controls described below. If nothing was recorded for a stay, add it manually rather than expecting GPS to recreate it.

To collect new history, start recording before your trip. Start enables detailed GPS and visit monitoring together; Stop ends both. Read the first-recording guide for permissions and recording controls.

2. Check the counting rules

Presence dates, rather than a continuous travel guarantee
SituationHow Whereday counts it
Many points in the same country on one dateOne day for that country, rather than one day per record.
Two countries on a travel dayThat date can count in both countries. Adding country totals may exceed the number of distinct dates.
A record near midnightThe resolved location’s timezone determines the local date, rather than UTC or your phone’s current timezone.
A completed visit with a verified departureIts local date range can contribute multiple dates.
An open visit or unverified departureOnly its arrival date is counted. Route points or manual stays can account for other dates.
Unknown country, GPS outlier, or recording gapNo invented dates or automatic continuous stay.
Overlapping manual and recorded presenceThe date counts once per country; the source flags can show both.

A checked example: four country dates across three dates

We passed invented records through the country-day calculator included in Whereday 1.7.16 (46), then saved and read back its CSV output. Country labels and timezones were assigned directly for this test; no coordinates were sent for geocoding and no real trip was recorded.

  • Portugal: two points on October 1, plus an overlapping manual stay from October 1 through October 2.
  • Spain: a point later on October 1, using Europe/Madrid.
  • France: a point at 22:30 UTC on October 2, using Europe/Paris. Its local date is October 3.
  • Excluded: an October 4 point flagged as an outlier, and an October 5 point without a country or timezone.

3. Add a stay that was not recorded

Use Add stay in Country Days. Choose the country and first and last dates, add an optional note, and save. Review the affected country’s dates afterwards. You can edit or delete a manual stay when you find a mistake.

A manual stay records your account of a trip; it does not create a GPS path. Keep tickets or other original records separately if you need them. Overlapping manual stays do not multiply the same country/date.

4. Identify countries in older locations

New recordings identify countries when network geocoding is enabled. Analyze saved locations checks earlier records in batches, with stop and resume controls. It can take time for a large archive.

Country identification uses Apple geocoding and follows your network-geocoding preference. A lookup sends coordinates to Apple; turning it off changes which unresolved locations can be assigned a country. Your journal remains on your device, and Whereday does not receive an archive of it. See the privacy policy for all network boundaries.

Analysis can identify a country for an existing coordinate. It cannot recover locations that were never recorded. Use a manual stay for dates you can account for independently.

5. Keep a CSV copy of your country dates

Use the export control in Country Days and save the CSV to a destination you choose. The file includes country codes, local dates, and recorded/manual source information. Viewing and editing your counts are free; writing the CSV follows the export access rules above. See current pricing.

The CSV uses country_code, local_date, and source. Source is recorded, manual, or recorded+manual; the last value still represents one country/date. Compare your file with the checked four-row example and inspect a known date and a travel day. A country-date CSV is a summary, not a backup of every visit, route point, or manual stay.
