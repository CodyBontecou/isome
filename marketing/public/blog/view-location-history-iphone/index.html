How to see your location history on iPhone

Check Apple Maps Visited Places, Google Maps Timeline, or a journal you already enabled. A new recorder can capture future trips, but cannot recover movement it never recorded.

By Cody Bontecou, iso.me’s developer · Reviewed October 26, 2026

Check the recorder you already used

To see where you have been on iPhone, check Apple Maps Visited Places, Google Maps Timeline, or a location journal you had already enabled. There is no single history screen that combines every app’s records. An empty day may mean nothing was recorded.

This guide covers your own visits and routes. If you want a record of future trips, you can start one in iso.me, but installing it cannot reconstruct earlier movement.

Directions you requested, places you searched for, and a recorded visit are different kinds of information. A search for a restaurant does not by itself establish when you arrived or how you travelled there.

Find visited places in Apple Maps

  1. Open Maps, then open Places.
  2. Choose Visited Places, if it is available.
  3. Search for a place or filter by date, category, or city. Use All Visits to browse.

Apple says this feature needs to be enabled before visits appear, is unavailable in some regions, and keeps visits encrypted so Apple cannot read them. Retention settings can also remove older entries. Menus vary with iOS version; use Apple’s Visited Places instructions for your version.

If you are looking for a setting called Significant Locations rather than a Maps list, check Apple’s Maps location settings. Do not expect every location-related setting to contain the same records.

Open Google Maps Timeline

  1. Open Google Maps and tap your profile picture or initial.
  2. Choose Your Timeline, then use the calendar to select a day.
  3. If history is missing, confirm which account and device you used. Check your backup and deletion settings before making changes.

Google Timeline stores history on the device and can keep an encrypted backup. To save an independent archive, Google’s iPhone instructions use Settings → Location & Privacy → Export Timeline data, then Save to Files. Settings are changing, so follow Google’s current Timeline guide if your menus differ.

Keep that archive separately. The verified App Store 1.7.16 build does not import Google Timeline files; the Google Timeline comparison explains the tradeoffs before you change your recording setup.

Why a day can be empty

First check the date, account, and app rather than assuming the history is gone. A map showing today may simply be the wrong view for last month’s trip. In iso.me, use Timeline’s date controls or change the Map date range.

  • Recording was off. A location permission lets an app request locations; it is not proof that the app recorded that day.
  • The record was removed. Look for retained files or supported backups before clearing more data or replacing a device.
  • The device missed part of the trip. Permission changes, a stopped session, GPS conditions, or a flat battery can leave gaps.
  • A visit has not appeared. Detected arrivals and departures are not an immediate check-in at every stop. A route can contain points even when a visit is missing.

For an incomplete iso.me day, use the missing visits and route points checklist to distinguish recording, display, Watch sync, and export issues.

Photos, receipts, and calendar entries may help you remember a day, but they are not a recovered GPS trace. A newly installed recorder cannot recreate coordinates it never captured.

Record your next day with iso.me

If you want a separate journal without an iso.me account, try a short recording before a longer trip:

  1. Install iso.me and complete permission setup. Always location access allows recording in the background.
  2. On Map, tap the recording control before you leave. Start enables GPS recording and visit monitoring together.
  3. Stop when you finish. Open Timeline and check the date, route, and times.
  4. If you need a portable route file, follow the iPhone route and GPX guide.

Recording and reviewing your journal are free. Writing export files requires the optional one-time unlock; preview is free. Read the setup walkthrough for permissions, battery controls, and keeping supported copies.

Core records are stored on your phone. Apple services supply place names and map features; default-on first-party onboarding telemetry excludes location records. Optional webhooks send records to your chosen endpoint. The privacy policy explains those boundaries.
