§ 06 FAQ

## Practical answers before you hand over location permission.

Location apps ask for meaningful trust. These answers keep the boundaries clear: what stays local, what Apple handles, what costs money, and how to leave with your data.

Will iso.me drain my battery?

iso.me uses iOS location services and gives you controls such as Start and Stop, distance filters, and auto-stop timers. High-accuracy route recording uses more battery, so use it for intentional sessions like trips, walks, hikes, or site visits.

Where is my location data stored?

Your visits and routes are stored locally on your iPhone. iso.me does not require an account and does not store your location history on iso.me servers.

Does iso.me use Apple geocoding?

It can, if you choose features that turn coordinates into readable place names or addresses. Those geocoding requests go through Apple's system service and are governed by Apple's privacy policies, not an iso.me location database.

Can I delete my location history?

Yes. Delete data from the app settings. Since iso.me does not keep a server copy, deleting the app also removes the local data from that device unless you exported or backed it up first.

Can I export or import my history?

Yes. Export supports JSON, CSV, Markdown, GeoJSON, GPX, KML, OwnTracks, and Overland for visits, route points, and outings. Import can restore a prior export, and importing is free.

Does iso.me work with Apple Watch?

Yes — and the Watch app records independently. It keeps its own GPS session, works without your iPhone nearby, and queues routes offline until it can sync them back. Watch complications also show today's visits, distance, and tracking status.

Does iso.me sync with iCloud?

No. iso.me stores your journal locally and does not sync through iCloud. Export and import are the portable backup path, and the Apple Watch syncs directly with your iPhone rather than through the cloud.

What is free and what is Pro?

The app is free to download; tracking, the timeline, the Watch app, widgets, and import are free. Lifetime export access covers all eight formats, scheduled automatic exports, and webhook delivery. It costs $19.99 for Individual or $39.99 for Family in the U.S. App Store, with a $19.99 Family Upgrade available to Individual owners. Local prices reflect purchasing power and may vary by storefront.

Does iso.me work with OwnTracks, Overland, or Dawarich?

iso.me can POST records in OwnTracks or Overland formats to a configured receiver. Compatibility depends on that receiver’s payload schema and authentication. Verify the protocol before enabling delivery; the Dawarich workflow is not yet verified here. Webhooks require Pro and are off by default. [Read the receiver requirements](https://isome.isolated.tech/docs/webhooks/).

What can Siri and Shortcuts do?

Start and stop tracking, read saved visits and outings, rename journal records, ask for today's stats, or export a chosen date range as a file. Use iso.me actions in Shortcuts; built-in Siri phrases cover recording, stats, renaming, and Export Data. Test a shortcut manually before scheduling it. See the [action list and export recipe](https://isome.isolated.tech/docs/siri-shortcuts/).

Can exports run automatically?

Yes. Auto Export saves your day to a folder you pick, once daily or as often as every hour, keeping one consistent dated file per day. Wake-ups are best effort: background refresh plus a scheduling service that sends timing-only pushes and never sees your location.

Is iso.me a Google Timeline alternative?

Yes. iso.me records your own visits and routes without a Google account. Google Timeline also stores history on your device and offers encrypted backup; iso.me differs in account requirements, export formats, and recording workflow. It does not import Google Timeline archives. [Read the comparison](https://isome.isolated.tech/compare/google-maps-timeline/).
