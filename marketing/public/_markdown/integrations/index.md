# Use your location records in another tool

Choose where your records go, check the result, and keep the original journal. File handoff and live delivery have different requirements.

By [Cody Bontecou](https://isolated.tech), iso.me’s developer · Reviewed October 26, 2026

## Choose a destination for the task

Your iso.me journal is stored on your iPhone. Copying a file or enabling webhooks sends records outside that local journal. The destination’s storage, access, and retention settings then apply.

### [Dawarich: send GPS points to your receiver](https://isome.isolated.tech/integrations/dawarich/)

Use iso.me’s optional Overland webhook with a matching Dawarich batch endpoint. The walkthrough includes a native three-point delivery test, checked database persistence, and a receiver screenshot. It separates a successful connection response from stored points. This is for people who already operate a receiver; Dawarich also offers its own tracking app.

### [Obsidian: read visits as a Markdown note](https://isome.isolated.tech/blog/iphone-location-journal-obsidian/)

Export a Markdown visit table and copy it into a desktop vault. The guide includes an actual exporter-generated sample, checked rendering, and navigation from a separate note. Mobile transfer, plugins, and scheduled vault delivery remain separate checks.

## Keep a copy you can verify

A readable note or a receiver’s points are not a complete backup of every iso.me setting, visit, or outing edit. Follow the [backup checklist](https://isome.isolated.tech/blog/back-up-iphone-location-history/) when your goal is restoration. Review the [format reference](https://isome.isolated.tech/docs/export-import/) and [privacy boundaries](https://isome.isolated.tech/privacy) before choosing a destination.
